UNDERSTANDING GLAUCOMA

The image can be focused, while visual signals are lost

Glaucoma damages retinal ganglion cells and the nerve fibers that carry their signals to the brain. In this example, the cornea and lens still focus light on the retina.

1 · Light → retinal image

The retina receives an inverted image. Its photoreceptors turn light into electrical activity.

2 · Retina → optic nerve → brain

Teal fibers and moving dots show an illustrative neural pathway. Gray fibers show damage. These are electrical signals, not light rays.

3 · Pressure and treatment

Aqueous fluid drains at the front of the eye. Elevated pressure is an important risk factor, but glaucoma also occurs at normal pressure. Eye drops, laser or surgery lower pressure to protect remaining vision.

Scientific notes & sources

Myopia and hyperopia use a simplified thin-eye model with limited accommodation. Paraxial refraction combines the cornea and lens at an equivalent optical plane with vitreous index 1.336 and a 24 mm retinal distance. The spectacle plane is included. Off-axis rays stop at the curved retina; focus behind it is shown only as a dashed extension. These are educational reduced-eye optics, not an individual ray-traced eye. Vision effects illustrate concepts and do not reproduce a particular person's vision. Hyperopia here means refractive long-sightedness, rather than age-related presbyopia. Glaucoma can occur at normal eye pressure; early disease may have no symptoms. Treatment does not restore lost vision. Cataract replacement shows an idealized outcome.
